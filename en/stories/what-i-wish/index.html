What I Wish Someone Had Told Me When I Was Diagnosed With Interstitial Cystitis

Ten things I learned in three years of living with IC and painful bladder syndrome, from someone who came out the other side.

By Miloš Stepanović · November 2, 2026 · 4 min read

When my urologist confirmed I had interstitial cystitis, I walked out of his office with a diagnosis and almost nothing else. No plan, no hope, and one sentence ringing in my ears: with this diagnosis, the future is hell.

I lived with IC from 2019 to 2022. Today I’m symptom-free. But if I could go back and sit next to the man I was on the day of that diagnosis, these are the things I would tell him.

If you’ve just been told you have interstitial cystitis, painful bladder syndrome, or bladder pain syndrome, this is for you.

1. You are not imagining it

For months before my diagnosis, every test came back “normal.” Sterile urine. Clean ultrasounds. Round after round of antibiotics that did nothing. At some point you start to wonder if it’s all in your head.

It isn’t. Interstitial cystitis is real, and the pain is real, even when the lab results say otherwise. A cystoscopy finally showed lesions across my bladder. Your pain doesn’t need a test result to be valid.

2. “Incurable” is a word, not a sentence

You will read the word incurable over and over. It will feel like a door slamming shut.

What I learned is that medicine often means “we don’t yet have a reliable treatment for everyone.” That’s not the same as “no one ever gets better.” People do get better. I’m one of them, and in my search I found others. Hold on to that.

3. Find a doctor who listens, and keep looking until you do

I saw many good, kind doctors who simply couldn’t help. The one who finally named my condition was the one who listened longest. Don’t stay with a doctor who dismisses you. You are allowed to ask for a second, third, or fourth opinion.

4. The pain will change how you see yourself. Protect your mind

I went 62 nights without real sleep. I lost my career and my marriage. There were moments I didn’t want to go on.

Nobody told me that chronic bladder pain would affect my mental health as much as my body. If you feel yourself slipping, tell someone. A friend, a family member, a doctor, a helpline. Asking for help is not weakness. It’s how you survive long enough to get better.

5. Look for people who have suffered, not only for experts

An old professor told me a saying I’ll never forget: “Wander among the doctors, but ask the one who has suffered.”

Experts know the condition. Other patients know the life. Some of the most useful things I learned came from people who had lived with IC, not from textbooks. Find the communities. Read the stories. Especially the ones that end well.

6. Pay attention to your body, every day

IC flares rarely come from nowhere. Over time I started noticing patterns: cold, stress, certain foods and drinks, lack of sleep. Keeping track of what makes your symptoms worse, and what gives you even a small break, is information no doctor can collect for you.

7. Small improvements count

When you’re in constant pain, a 10% improvement can feel like nothing. It isn’t. My first real sign of hope was a single night of full sleep. Write the good days down. On the bad days, you’ll need proof that they exist.

8. Be suspicious of quick fixes, but don’t close your mind

You will find a thousand products promising to cure interstitial cystitis overnight. Most of them will take your money and give you nothing.

At the same time, don’t close your mind entirely. What finally changed things for me wasn’t a miracle product. It was a long, demanding road, with a lot of discipline and a lot of mistakes. Be careful, ask questions, and talk to your doctor before you try anything significant.

9. The people around you are suffering too

My father worked day and night through all of it, paying for one more chance for his son. My family carried me when I couldn’t carry myself.

Let the people who love you in. Tell them what you need. And when you get better, and I hope you will, tell them thank you.

10. Recovery from IC is possible

Two years into my search, I found a post from a young woman on the other side of the world. She had IC, and she’d been thinking about ending her life. Two months later she wrote three words: “I’M HEALED.”

That post didn’t fix my bladder. But it gave me the one thing I’d lost: proof that it could be done.

Today I sleep through the night. I walk barefoot on cold tiles. I’m a real dad to my son again.
