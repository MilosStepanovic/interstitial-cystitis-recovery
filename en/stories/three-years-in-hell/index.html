Interstitial Cystitis Recovery: My Three Years in Hell, and the Day the Pain Switched Off

How my IC recovery actually unfolded, from the first infection to the day the pain stopped.

By Miloš Stepanović · November 1, 2026 · 5 min read

I lived with interstitial cystitis from 2019 to 2022. For three years I went through hell trying to find a way out.

When it was finally over, I didn’t want to return to IC, not even in my thoughts. But I know what it’s like to type “can interstitial cystitis be cured” into a search bar at three in the morning, and to find nothing but the word incurable. So I’m writing this for the person doing that tonight.

It started as an ordinary bladder infection

In the winter of 2019 I caught what I thought was a simple bladder infection. As a boy I’d had a few, and they always passed with a hot water bottle on my lower belly.

This one never went away.

I live in Serbia, and I had access to good doctors. Their concern was honest and human. It just didn’t help. Ultrasounds. Detailed lab work. Sterile urine, every single time. Round after round of antibiotics. After three months I went to the best urologists in my country. Fancier machines, stronger-sounding medicines, the same result.

The diagnosis: interstitial cystitis

Eventually I ended up with an older professor of urology. At our first appointment, he asked me whether we should talk as doctor and patient, or as two Christians. I said as Christians.

“From everything I’m hearing,” he said, “you have interstitial cystitis. And with that diagnosis, the future is… in Christian terms, hell.”

The cystoscopy confirmed it: Hunner-type lesions across the inside of my bladder, traces of blood, the lining badly damaged, the urethra narrowed by swelling. Interstitial cystitis, also called painful bladder syndrome or bladder pain syndrome. A chronic condition that, as far as medicine was concerned, could at best be managed.

What living with IC actually feels like

If you have IC, you already know. If you don’t, I’m not sure I can explain it.

The constant urge to urinate, with only a few drops to show for it. Burning. Stabbing. The feeling of sandpaper on the most delicate tissue. I once described an IC flare as someone pouring vinegar, nonstop, into a deep open wound, and even that felt too gentle. All day, every day. No break. No relief.

I went 62 nights without real sleep. I lost my career. My marriage ended. My little boy had a father who was mostly lying down. There were moments when I didn’t want to go on.

“Ask the one who has suffered”

Before I left his office, that professor told me an old saying: “Wander among the doctors, but ask the one who has suffered.”

That sentence changed how I searched. I had already tried almost everything, conventional and alternative. I didn’t need another pill that promised to “improve function” by two percent. I needed one living person who had been exactly where I was and had come out the other side.

I started noticing something. Every small improvement other IC patients mentioned, however modest, had come after a change in what they ate. That became my thread to pull.

Searching for a living person who recovered

It took me two years.

Then, late one night, in a corner of the internet I’d never thought to look, I found a post from a young woman on the other side of the world. She had interstitial cystitis. She wrote that the pain was destroying her and that she’d been thinking about ending her life.

My heart sank. Another story like mine.

Then I scrolled down. Two months later, the same young woman had written three words: “I’M HEALED.”

I cried like a child. The only other time in my life I had cried from happiness was the day my son was born.

I went to my father, who had worked day and night through all of it to pay for one more chance for his son. “Dad, I’m going to get well,” I said. Without looking up from his desk, he answered: “I’ve known that from the start.” When I told him I had found a living person who had recovered from IC, he looked at me and said only this: “Now go all the way, champ.”

The road to interstitial cystitis recovery

What came after wasn’t one magic pill or one single change. It was a long road: a different way of eating and living, wrong turns, flares that frightened me, and strangers across the world who helped me for nothing. It demanded discipline I didn’t know I had.

And then one day, the pain switched off. Like pressing a button. All of it: the burning, the urgency, the collapse, the dark moments.

That was a few years ago. It has never come back.

Life after IC

Today I sleep through the night. I walk barefoot on cold tiles, something I was sure I would never do again. I eat whatever I want. And I’m a real dad again, not a “dad from the sickbed.”

Since then, the approach I followed has helped many other people I’ve worked with. But it’s impossible to stay available to everyone in the way each person needs. People also want to see the clear steps of someone’s success with their own eyes. And I feel a quiet call of conscience to share it.

Why I wrote it all down

So I wrote the whole story down in a book: the doctors, the dead ends, the people who helped me, and exactly what I did, step by step, with the mistakes I’d want others to avoid.

It is not medical literature. It’s my personal testimony. Every case of interstitial cystitis is different, and I’d always encourage you to talk to your own doctor before changing anything about your health.
