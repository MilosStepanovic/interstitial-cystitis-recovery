My story, in short

In the winter of 2019–2020 I got a bladder infection that would not go away. Then came urologists in two cities, ultrasounds, lab work, mountains of antibiotics and finally a cystoscopy: lesions across the bladder. The diagnosis was interstitial cystitis.

The illness cost me my career, my marriage and very nearly my life. Instead of another medicine, I started looking for a living person who had recovered. I found one in a Facebook group, on the other side of the world.

Then came raw veganism, juice fasting and finally a dry fast, which I did with guidance from someone experienced. Today I am healthy, I eat everything, and I live a normal life.

Chapter I — Introduction

This book very nearly dreamed itself into nothingness in my mind and heart. It almost never got written down.

That it is now recorded and published owes something to that near-loss, but also to a purpose: to reach out to, and help, the people who don’t merely suffer from this illness but live its hell in the first person.

Doctors will tell you it won’t kill you — and yet you have never been closer to death, or to ending up there by your own hand. Research shows that a high percentage of patients take their own lives within the first three months of the illness. The rest usually aren’t followed at all, because they simply stop going to doctors, and a pale, incomplete statistic quietly counts them among the living.

So, lest I forget while the memory is still fresh: on one side, my own tragedy, which cost me my career, my marriage, and very nearly my life; on the other, a debt to everyone who suffers from the same affliction and feels the ground giving way beneath them. Hence the pages that follow.

All of this could easily be turned into a novel — just as each of you, my dear readers, could write a novel about your own ordeal. Yet precisely for your sake, I will set it in a blend of ¹what was truly lived and ²what is technically needed to make it a thing of the past.

This book, then, will be something between an honest confession and a practical guide: how to make IC a thing of the past.

Chapter II — The Onset: An Unusually Severe, Stubborn Bladder Infection

In the winter of 2019–2020, after more than fifteen years, a bladder infection caught up with me. I admit I had been a little careless, dressing in clothes that weren’t really suited to working outdoors in winter. Then again, that winter wasn’t like the ones before it — it was noticeably warmer and milder — but it was clearly cold enough to set it off.

As a boy, I’d had a few bladder infections, but only acute ones, and they passed almost the moment my mother pressed a bottle of hot water to my lower belly. Still, that pain stayed etched in my memory from childhood, because only someone who has ever had a bladder infection — even one that isn’t interstitial cystitis — knows how much a bladder hurts when it becomes “inflamed.”

I assumed it was something acute again, something a hot-water bottle (a bit of local warmth) would fix. But this time, the thing simply wouldn’t go away…

Questions

What is interstitial cystitis?

Interstitial cystitis (IC), also called painful bladder syndrome or bladder pain syndrome, is a chronic condition with bladder pain and pressure and a frequent urge to urinate. Urine tests are usually sterile, so antibiotics don’t help.

Is this a medical book?

No. It is my personal experience and what helped me and the people I have worked with. It does not replace a doctor. The book has a dedicated safety section to read before any longer fast.
